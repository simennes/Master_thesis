Uncertainty in GP accuracy

Assessing the uncertainty in the GP accuracy ˆr is straightforward in our within-population models.

We can use ˆσCV to estimate the uncertainty, where ˆσCV is the sample standard deviation over the

been enough to take joint posterior samples of the genetic value vector g from a given across-population

In the main text we assessed the impact of population-differences in relatedness, LD, and fixation

symmetric. That is, flipping which population is treated as (1) or (2) would result in a different result

for the genetic correlations (which is apparent in the results for Scenario 3, see below). Thirdly, as

pointed out in the main text, fitting these bivariate models relies on phenotypic measurements from

estimates of across-population GP accuracy: the phenotypes in the test set could be used to train a GP
